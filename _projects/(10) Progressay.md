## The problem

Marking essays is slow, and it is not consistent. The same piece of writing scored by two teachers — or by one teacher on Monday and Friday — can land differently. Automating it means building something that disagrees with a human marker no more than two humans disagree with each other.

## The approach

An NLP pipeline over free-form student writing: structure, coherence, vocabulary and topical relevance, scored against a rubric rather than a single opaque number. The output has to be *explainable* — a grade a teacher can't interrogate is a grade they won't use.

## What was hard

**Trust, not accuracy.** The bar isn't a benchmark score; it's whether a teacher will accept the mark without re-reading the essay. That pushes everything toward showing the reasoning rather than just the result.

**Consistency across cohorts.** A model that drifts between classes, year groups or prompts is worse than no model, because inconsistency is exactly the problem it was brought in to solve.
